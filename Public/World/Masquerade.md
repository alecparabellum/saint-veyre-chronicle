# Masquerade

## Product Information

**House:** Maison Le Veil  
**Perfumer:** Alec Le Veil  
**Unofficial Name:** Rose Red  
**Concentrations:** Eau de Parfum and Extrait de Parfum  
**Primary Presentation:** 100 ml  
**Fragrance Family:** Fruity, rose, amber, gourmand, woody, oud  
**Market Position:** Prestige unisex fragrance  
**Seasonality:** Autumn, winter, and evening wear  
**Status:** Strong commercial success and Maison Le Veil's most popular fragrance among women  

---

# Overview

**Masquerade** is the warmest, sweetest, strongest, and most theatrical fragrance in The Le Veil Collection.

It combines grapefruit and blood orange with strawberry, raspberry, rose, oud, amber, vanilla, benzoin, caramel, cedar, and musk.

The fragrance begins with a fresh burst of tart citrus and red fruit.

Its freshness is short-lived.

The fruit quickly becomes sweeter, darker, and more saturated as it merges with a dense rose heart.

The drydown replaces much of the opening brightness with amber, vanilla, benzoin, caramel, musk, wood, and restrained oud.

Some of the tart red-fruit character remains until the end.

---

# Position within The Le Veil Collection

Masquerade followed Le Masque Eau de Parfum.

It uses the collection's grapefruit, rose, and musk backbone but interprets it through:

- Red fruit.
- Amber.
- Vanilla.
- Caramel.
- Oud.
- Night.
- Seduction.
- Performance.
- Theatricality.

Where La Rose Bleue Voilée is controlled and versatile, Masquerade is deliberately indulgent.

Where Le Masque creates intimacy through cleanliness, Masquerade creates intimacy through heat, sweetness, and excess.

---

# Unofficial Rose Red Identity

Masquerade is sometimes called:

**Rose Red**

Maison Le Veil never officially adopted the name.

The association developed after blue roses became central to La Rose Bleue Voilée marketing.

Masquerade campaigns increasingly used:

- Red roses.
- Blood orange.
- Red berries.
- Burgundy velvet.
- Ruby liquid.
- Dark gold.
- Masquerade masks.
- Candlelight.

Consumers began treating it as the red fragrance of the collection.

The name became standard among dupe houses but remained unofficial within Maison Le Veil.

# Opening

## Principal Materials

- Grapefruit.
- Blood orange.
- Strawberry.
- Raspberry.

The opening is the freshest stage of the fragrance.

Grapefruit provides bitterness.

Blood orange gives bright citrus sweetness and a deep red character.

Strawberry adds soft sweetness.

Raspberry provides tartness and a darker fruit effect.

The opening is vivid, juicy, and immediately attractive.

It does not remain light for long.

---

# Heart

## Principal Materials

- Bulgarian rose.
- Turkish rose.
- Strawberry.
- Raspberry.
- Amber.

The roses are the centre of Masquerade.

Bulgarian rose provides richness and depth.

Turkish rose gives a darker, spiced, almost wine-like quality.

The red fruits merge with the petals.

Strawberry contributes sweetness.

Raspberry preserves tartness and prevents the floral heart from becoming entirely syrupy.

Amber begins to darken the composition.

The heart is lush, warm, and highly diffusive.

---

# Drydown

## Principal Materials

- Agarwood.
- Amber.
- Vanilla.
- Benzoin.
- Caramel.
- Cedar.
- Musk.

The drydown becomes significantly darker and sweeter.

Amber and benzoin form a resinous body.

Vanilla softens the rose and fruit.

Caramel deepens the sweetness without turning the composition into a simple dessert fragrance.

Cedar provides structure.

Musk extends the scent trail.

Oud remains present beneath the composition.

In the Eau de Parfum it is controlled and deliberately subtle, giving depth and expense without dominating the fragrance.

The final effect is a dark sweet rose with persistent red-fruit tartness.

---

# Eau de Parfum

## Character

The Eau de Parfum is considered the balanced version of Masquerade.

It provides:

- Strong performance.
- Dense rose.
- Clear red-fruit character.
- Noticeable sweetness.
- Subtle oud.
- Warm amber.
- A smooth gourmand drydown.

---

## Liquid

The EDP liquid is transparent blood red.

It should appear rich and jewel-like while remaining transparent enough for light to pass through the ribbed bottle.

---

## Wearability

The EDP is best suited to:

- Autumn.
- Winter.
- Night.
- Formal events.
- Dates.
- Parties.
- Luxury hotels.
- Evening wear.
- Situations where strong projection is desirable.

It may be worn by any gender.

Its greatest commercial popularity is among women.

# Extrait de Parfum

## Character

Masquerade Extrait is darker, heavier, and more oud-forward.

It increases:

- Concentration.
- Projection.
- Longevity.
- Rose density.
- Amber.
- Benzoin.
- Oud.
- Musk.

The addition of violet gives the floral heart a shadowed quality.

The Extrait is less fruity in balance than the EDP.

Its opening still contains red fruit and citrus, but the darker materials appear more quickly and remain more dominant.

---

## Market Motivation

The Extrait was introduced after repeated demand for:

- More oud.
- Greater strength.
- Longer performance.
- A darker presentation.
- A more expensive and concentrated edition.

It was positioned as the most opulent release within The Le Veil Collection.

---

## Price

The Extrait is the most expensive release within the collection.

Its price reflects:

- Higher concentration.
- Greater use of oud.
- Increased rose materials.
- More expensive resins.
- Prestige positioning.
- Darker collector packaging.

---

## Packaging

Even at night, campaigns retain:

## Eau de Parfum Human Campaign

The principal human advertisement features a glamorous Black woman seated on the edge of a bed in a luxury penthouse at night.

The penthouse remains bright.

Its walls are white or ivory, with red details throughout the room.

The woman wears:

- A deep red evening dress.
- Stiletto shoes.
- Gold jewellery.

She sits with crossed legs and looks directly at the camera with a provocative smile.

She holds:

- Masquerade Eau de Parfum in one hand.
- A gold mask in the other.

Behind her, a white woman appears to be asleep on her stomach.

Her face is not visible.

A second mask rests on the bed.

The image implies an intimate encounter without showing nudity or explicit sexual activity.

---

## Tabloid Interpretation

Some tabloids claimed that the Black woman in the campaign was intended as a stand-in for Alec herself.

The advertisement's implied relationship and knowing expression encouraged speculation about the founder's private life.

Maison Le Veil never confirmed the interpretation.

The ambiguity benefited the campaign.

## Human Campaign

The principal Extrait advertisement features a long-haired man at an evening masquerade party.

He wears:

- Black formal tailoring.
- A dark ornate mask.
- Subtle red detailing.

He holds the fragrance.

Three women compete for his attention.

Their proximity, posture, and eye contact create the sense that the fragrance has made him the centre of the event.

**Richer oud. Deeper rose. Violet dusk.**

**After dark.**

# Commercial Reception

Masquerade sells more than Le Masque but less than La Rose Bleue Voilée.

Its popularity is driven by:

- Strong performance.
- Sweetness.
- Red-fruit opening.
- Rich rose.
- Luxurious packaging.
- Night-time character.
- High compliment factor.
- Unisex wearability.
- Strong appeal among women.

It is Maison Le Veil's most popular fragrance with the female audience.

---

# Critical Reception

Reviewers praise:

- The tartness of the red fruits.
- The central rose accord.
- The restraint of the oud in the EDP.
- The transition from brightness to dark sweetness.
- The balance between gourmand and floral elements.
- Its performance.
- Its ability to remain unisex despite sweetness.

Criticism often focuses upon:

- The price.
- The density of the drydown.
- The Extrait's increased oud.
- The reduction of contrast in the stronger concentration.
- The possibility of overspraying.

---

# Dupe Market

Imitation houses commonly market Masquerade copies as:

**Rose Red**

These interpretations usually emphasise:

- Raspberry.
- Strawberry.
- Rose.
- Vanilla.
- Caramel.
- Oud.

Many copies become sweeter and louder than the original.

They often lose:

- Grapefruit bitterness.
- Blood-orange freshness.
- Tartness that persists through the drydown.
- Controlled oud.
- The distinction between fruit sweetness and resinous sweetness.
- The transition between stages.

---

# Legacy

Masquerade expanded the emotional and commercial range of Maison Le Veil.

It became:

- The house's principal nocturnal fragrance.
- Its most popular release among women.
- The red member of the unofficial Rose Blue, Rose White, and Rose Red trio.
- The source of Maison Le Veil's most provocative advertising.
- Proof that the house could embrace sweetness and theatricality without abandoning structure.
