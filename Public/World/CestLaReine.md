# C'est La Reine

## Overview

**C'est La Reine** is a prestigious French fragrance house founded by celebrated perfumer **Céleste Reine**.

The company built its reputation through elegant feminine perfumes inspired by French court culture, Parisian sophistication, and the symbolic language of royalty.

For much of its early history, the house was respected within the luxury fragrance industry but remained relatively specialised. Its perfumes attracted devoted clients and critical praise, yet the company repeatedly failed to establish a meaningful presence in the men's fragrance market.

That changed with the release of:

**La Reine de Versailles Pour Homme**

Created by the young perfumer Alexandra Jean, later professionally known as Alec Le Veil, the fragrance became C'est La Reine's first major masculine success and transformed the company from a respected niche house into an internationally recognised luxury brand.

---

## House Philosophy

C'est La Reine presents fragrance as an expression of personal sovereignty.

Its perfumes are not advertised merely as pleasant accessories. They are portrayed as declarations of identity, confidence, desire, refinement, and social presence.

The house associates scent with:

* Royal authority.
* Personal ambition.
* Seduction.
* Heritage.
* Ceremony.
* Self-possession.
* The power to define how one is remembered.

C'est La Reine advertising often treats the act of wearing perfume as equivalent to placing a crown upon oneself.

---

## Brand Positioning

C'est La Reine occupies the upper end of the luxury fragrance market.

The house combines:

* French perfumery tradition.
* Versailles-inspired grandeur.
* Parisian fashion sensibility.
* Ornate historical references.
* Modern luxury photography.
* Restrained theatricality.

The brand is regal without becoming fanciful.

Its visual identity suggests inherited power, even when its customers have built their own fortunes.

---

# Visual Identity

## Primary Emblem

The primary C'est La Reine emblem is an ornate royal crest.

At its centre is a single capital:

**R**

The letter is enclosed within a narrow oval frame and surrounded by symmetrical baroque foliage, scrollwork, and decorative flourishes.

A small crown sits above the oval.

The use of the solitary **R** allows the emblem to represent several ideas simultaneously:

* Reine.
* Royalty.
* Reign.
* Reputation.
* The authority of the wearer.

The emblem is normally rendered in antique gold against a black field.

---

## Primary Wordmark

The principal wordmark reads:

**C'EST LA REINE**

The lettering uses a high-contrast classical serif with wide spacing and a formal editorial quality.

The wordmark is usually accompanied by:

**PARIS**

Paris appears beneath the house name in smaller, widely spaced capital letters.

The crest may appear above the wordmark or to its left, depending upon the format.

---

## Secondary Marks

Approved secondary marks include:

### The Crown

A small, simplified royal crown used on bottle caps, seals, paper goods, and campaign dividers.

### The Oval R

The central **R** enclosed within its oval frame without the surrounding crest.

This form is used when the complete emblem would be too detailed.

### The Standalone R

A single ornamental **R**, primarily used for bottle hardware, wax seals, atomiser caps, and repeating patterns.

### The Royal Flourish

A curved botanical ornament derived from the full crest.

It may be used as a border, corner decoration, or separator.

# Colour Palette

## Reine Black

**Hex:** `#0B0B0B`

The primary brand colour.

Used for labels, cartons, advertising backgrounds, shopping bags, and presentation cases.

It should appear deep and warm rather than flat or blue-black.

---

## Noir Satin

**Hex:** `#1C1C1C`

A softer secondary black used to create layered black-on-black surfaces.

Common applications include satin ribbons, embossed patterns, and interior packaging.

---

## Antique Crown Gold

**Hex:** `#B68D2E`

The principal metallic colour.

Used for the crest, wordmark, borders, seals, and hardware.

It should resemble aged jewellery or gilded furniture rather than bright yellow metal.

---

## Champagne Gold

**Hex:** `#E1C78A`

A lighter gold used for highlights, fine rules, secondary lettering, and reflective surfaces.

---

## Reine Ivory

**Hex:** `#F6F2E9`

Used sparingly for correspondence, archival advertising, scent cards, and editorial campaigns.

# Typography

## Display Typeface

**Cinzel**

Used for:

* Product names.
* House titles.
* Campaign headlines.
* Formal packaging.
* Prestige advertising.

The typeface reinforces the brand's historical and architectural character.

---

## Supporting Typeface

**Montserrat**

Used for:

* Product information.
* Concentration and volume.
* Retail descriptions.
* Ingredient listings.
* Corporate communications.
* Small supporting copy.

Its clean geometry prevents the identity from becoming excessively ornamental.

---

## Typographic Character

C'est La Reine typography should feel:

* Formal.
* Spacious.
* Architectural.
* Editorial.
* Controlled.
* Expensive.

Large areas of negative space are preferred.

Gold lettering is never crowded.

---

# Materials and Finishes

The house commonly uses:

* Heavy clear or amber-tinted glass.
* Black lacquer.
* Textured black paper.
* Gold foil.
* Gold embossing.
* Black satin ribbon.
* Crystal-like faceted caps.
* Dark marble.
* Ivory archival paper.
* Wax seals.
* Rigid presentation boxes.

Packaging should feel substantial in the hand.

Weight is an important part of the brand experience. Bottles and boxes are designed to resemble permanent objects rather than disposable containers.

---

# Brand Voice

C'est La Reine speaks with confidence and restraint.

Its copy rarely explains itself at length. Headlines are short, declarative, and constructed around power, identity, memory, and desire.

The brand does not use casual humour or exaggerated enthusiasm.

It assumes the customer understands luxury.

---

## Principal Brand Lines

### House Motto

**Wear Your Crown.**

### Brand Promise

**Every scent tells a story. Yours should reign.**

### Traditional Feminine Line

**Fragrances for the modern queen.**

The feminine line remains associated with the house's historical identity, although the success of La Reine de Versailles Pour Homme broadened the meaning of the crown beyond gender.

---

# Masculine Fragrance Identity

Before La Reine de Versailles Pour Homme, C'est La Reine's masculine releases had struggled to distinguish themselves from the house's feminine catalogue.

They were often criticised for feeling like adaptations of existing perfumes rather than fragrances created around a specifically masculine identity.

Alexandra Jean proposed a different approach.

Rather than making the brand less regal, she made its idea of royalty more severe.

The resulting masculine language emphasised:

* Composure rather than aggression.
* Authority rather than noise.
* Sexual confidence rather than adolescent provocation.
* Maturity rather than age.
* Presence rather than performance.
* Earned status rather than inherited entitlement.

The crown remained central.

Its meaning changed from decorative femininity to command.

---

# The Alexandra Jean Collaboration

Alexandra Jean joined C'est La Reine as an outside collaborator while still in her twenties.

Although relatively young, she had already developed a reputation for combining artistic experimentation with commercial instinct.

C'est La Reine hired her to address the house's continued failure in masculine fragrance.

Rather than accepting a conventional fee, Alexandra negotiated a royalty agreement tied to the commercial performance of her creation.

The decision was considered unusually bold for a perfumer of her age and professional standing.

La Reine de Versailles Pour Homme exceeded all internal expectations.

The agreement made Alexandra independently wealthy and established her as one of the most discussed young perfumers in the industry.

It also created a lasting financial and personal tension between Alexandra and C'est La Reine.

---

# Brand Legacy

C'est La Reine remains closely associated with feminine luxury, but La Reine de Versailles Pour Homme permanently altered its reputation.

The fragrance proved that the house's royal identity could appeal to men without abandoning its established character.

It also became inseparable from the public mythology surrounding Alec Le Veil.

For C'est La Reine, the perfume represents its greatest expansion.

For Alec, it represents the moment talent became power.

For the fragrance industry, it remains the product that made both names impossible to ignore.
