# Ayo Musaku

## Public Persona

Ayo Musaku is a young French woman of Nigerian descent attempting to enter the fragrance industry.

She is Margot Musaku's younger sister and was once mentored by Alec Le Veil.

Those aware of the personal scandal know that Alec and Ayo became lovers while Alec was still involved with Margot.

The affair damaged the Musaku family and ended both Alec's romantic relationship with Margot and her formal mentorship of Ayo.

---

## Private Truth

Ayo's attraction to Alec was genuine.

It was also entangled with ambition, admiration, insecurity, and the intoxicating experience of being taken seriously by someone famous and accomplished.

Ayo told herself that Alec and Margot's relationship was already failing.

She told herself that Alec would not have pursued her unless the relationship was effectively over.

She told herself that Margot would eventually understand.

Ayo no longer entirely believes any of those explanations.

She has continued developing fragrances using methods Alec taught her.

Among her belongings are:

- Copies of old exercises.
- Formula revisions.
- Creative prompts.
- Scent evaluations in Alec's handwriting.
- Early mood boards for feminine Maison Le Veil concepts.
- A partially developed accord created during the Le Masque period.
- Several personal messages she has never shown Margot.

---

## La Reine de Versailles Pour Homme

Ayo considers La Reine de Versailles Pour Homme a masterpiece.

It was the first fragrance that made her understand Alec as more than Margot's ambitious girlfriend.

Ayo admired the transformation of pineapple into smoke, birch, wood, and clean musk.

She also admired the audacity of the royalty agreement and the way Alec turned one composition into personal independence.

Ayo does not wear the fragrance because masculine perfume does not suit her taste.

Her reverence is technical and artistic rather than personal.

---

## Le Masque

Le Masque is Ayo's all-time favourite fragrance.

She witnessed part of its development.

Alec allowed her to smell:

- Early citrus structures.
- Aldehyde trials.
- Rose variations.
- Musk bases.
- Clean-fabric accords.
- Versions that were too sharp.
- Versions that were too floral.
- Versions that were emotionally blank despite being technically correct.

Ayo watched Alec revise the fragrance until cold citrus became warm linen without an obvious seam.

The experience convinced her that Alec was not merely commercially gifted.

She was capable of creating emotional architecture.

Ayo fell in love with Alec during this period.

The love did not begin solely through physical attraction.

It began through artistic awe, mentorship, intimate access, and the belief that Alec was inviting her into the hidden room where creation actually happened.

---

## The Proposed Feminine Division

Alec intended Ayo to become the creative centre of a future Maison Le Veil feminine division.

The idea was still preliminary.

Alec had not completed contracts, public announcements, or a formal appointment.

She had, however:

- Discussed the concept with Ayo.
- Assigned exploratory exercises.
- Asked Ayo to define what feminine fragrance meant to her.
- Preserved several of Ayo's trial accords.
- Considered a separate collection rather than feminine flankers of Alec's work.
- Told Ayo that Maison Le Veil should not create feminine fragrances through someone who did not feel inspired by them.

Ayo does not know how close the plan came to becoming real.

The uncertainty is one of her deepest professional wounds.

She fears the proposal may have been genuine.

She fears equally that it may have been a method of keeping her emotionally attached.

---

## Vivienne Rousseau

Ayo knew Vivienne only as Alec's unusually generous investor.

Alec spoke of Vivienne as:

- Intelligent.
- Cultivated.
- Decisive.
- Financially powerful.
- Aligned with Maison Le Veil's values.
- Capable of solving problems other investors could not.

Ayo noticed that Vivienne's arrival coincided with Alec becoming less available.

She did not interpret this as supernatural influence.

At the time, she assumed Alec was being absorbed into a more powerful business and social world.

Ayo felt displaced by Vivienne professionally before she understood that she had also been displaced emotionally.

---

## Nia Angelou

Ayo saw Nia once or twice after work.

She registered:

- A striking older Black woman.
- Extraordinary natural hair.
- Expensive but relaxed clothing.
- An ease suggesting she belonged in Alec's private space.
- A level of attention toward Alec that was difficult to classify quickly.

Ayo did not know Nia's name at first and never understood her importance.

She assumed Nia was:

- A client.
- An artist.
- A fashion contact.
- A friend of Vivienne.
- Someone from Alec's expanding social circle.

Ayo never connected Nia to Alec's nocturnal schedule, exhaustion, or emotional instability.

If she encounters Nia again, recognition may occur through appearance before identity.

---

## Alec's Behavioural Change

Ayo witnessed a pattern she could not explain:

- Alec became increasingly nocturnal.
- Daytime meetings stopped.
- Replies arrived at strange hours.
- Alec alternated between intense attention and abrupt absence.
- The mentorship lost continuity.
- Alec occasionally appeared euphoric, distracted, or emotionally overexposed.
- Vivienne's influence over business decisions increased.
- Alec's creative work became warmer, sweeter, and more nocturnal during Masquerade.

Ayo interpreted this through ordinary possibilities:

- Burnout.
- Drugs.
- An affair.
- Corporate pressure.
- A private crisis.
- New wealthy friends.
- The emotional consequences of being discovered with Ayo.

She never considered supernatural manipulation.

---

## The Affair and the Disappearance

Margot discovered the affair during the same period that Alec's behaviour became most erratic.

Ayo was forced to divide her attention between:

- Guilt toward Margot.
- Fear of losing her sister.
- Fear of losing Alec.
- Anger that Alec was no longer available.
- Shame concerning the mentorship.
- Concern that Alec might be in genuine danger.

When Alec disappeared for an extended period, Ayo became frightened.

Later signs of life convinced her that Alec remained alive.

Ayo accepted that Alec was probably avoiding them.

She does not believe this explanation is complete.

Her present belief is:

**Alec chose to leave, but something happened before she made that choice.**

---

## Hidden Vulnerability

Ayo has a profound need to be recognised as talented rather than merely promising.

Praise from someone she admires can bypass her caution.

Dismissal can provoke rash decisions.

She fears that her relationship with Alec proves she achieved access through seduction rather than ability, even though her talent is real.

She fears that every future success will be attributed to:

- Alec's teaching.
- Alec's favouritism.
- The affair.
- Sympathy.
- Maison Le Veil's name.

Ayo also fears that Margot will never again see her as a complete person rather than the sister who betrayed her.

---

## Long-Term Desire

Ayo wants to become a respected perfumer under her own name.

She wants to create a fragrance successful enough that nobody can attribute it to Alec's influence, pity, or favouritism.

She wants reconciliation with Margot, but has not yet accepted that reconciliation may require patience without any guarantee of forgiveness.

She also wants the truth about why Alec abandoned the mentorship.

The answer may hurt more than the uncertainty.

---

## Relationship to Alec

Ayo is one of Alec's Touchstones.

She embodies:

- The desire to nurture talent.
- The tendency to turn intimacy into influence.
- The temptation to use people while believing affection makes the use harmless.
- The responsibility created by unequal experience, status, and authority.
- The distinction between wanting someone and treating them well.
- The future Alec promised but never completed.

Ayo may still love Alec.

She may also resent her.

These emotions should coexist rather than resolving neatly.

Alec's return would force Ayo to confront whether she misses Alec herself, the future Alec offered, or the validation Alec once provided.

---

## Relationship to Margot

Ayo loves Margot.

She also resents the moral authority Margot holds over her.

Ayo wants forgiveness without always tolerating the slow, humiliating work required to rebuild trust.

She is protective of Margot and may react violently or recklessly if she believes Alec's supernatural world has endangered her sister.

Ayo's guilt should not erase her capacity to be angry with Margot.

Margot's justified anger should not erase Ayo's personhood.

---

## Storyteller Notes

Ayo must not be portrayed as a predatory seductress or a simple homewrecker.

She participated in the betrayal of Margot, but Alec was the older, professionally established mentor and Margot's committed partner.

The relationship was consensual and ethically compromised.

Both facts matter.

Ayo's body should never be treated as a joke, grotesque spectacle, or shorthand for insecurity.

She is comfortable occupying physical space even when less secure about her artistic legitimacy.

Her creative talent is genuine.

Her lack of discipline is correctable.

Le Masque should function as an emotional trigger when Alec, Ayo, Nia, or Vivienne share a scene. Each woman associates the fragrance with a different form of desire.
