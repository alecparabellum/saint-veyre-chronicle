# Céleste Reine

## Basic Information

**Name:** Céleste Reine  
**Nature:** Mortal  
**Nationality:** French  
**Age:** Early Fifties  
**Occupation:** Founder and Chief Executive Officer of C'est La Reine  
**Primary Base:** Paris, France  
**Relationship to Alec:** Former employer, commercial rival, hidden architect of the assassination attempt  

Céleste has no supernatural nature and possessed no reliable knowledge of Kindred society during Alec's mortal life.

She believed Vivienne Rousseau to be an unusually wealthy, connected, and formidable investor.

She did not know that she was contesting corporate influence with a Ventrue.

---

## Public Persona

Céleste Reine is one of the most respected executives in French luxury fragrance.

She is known publicly for:

- Discipline.
- Strategic intelligence.
- High standards.
- Controlled elegance.
- Recognising commercially useful talent.
- Building C'est La Reine from a respected feminine house into an international prestige brand.
- Protecting the company's visual and historical identity.
- Remaining composed during public controversy.

Céleste presents herself as the custodian of a legacy rather than merely the owner of a company.

She speaks of C'est La Reine as an institution that must survive individual careers, temporary trends, and personal disagreements.

Her public image is stern but not cruel.

Industry professionals often describe her as intimidating, demanding, and fair to people who meet her standards.

---

## Céleste's Original Feelings toward Alexandra

Céleste genuinely liked Alexandra Jean.

She recognised that Alexandra was ambitious, talented, commercially intuitive, and unusually capable of translating technical perfumery into a clear public identity.

Céleste also underestimated her.

When Alexandra requested royalties for La Reine de Versailles Pour Homme, Céleste accepted because:

- C'est La Reine had repeatedly failed in masculine fragrance.
- The perfume was still an uncertain project.
- Alexandra was young.
- Céleste expected Alexandra to remain dependent upon the house.
- Céleste intended to place her in charge of a future masculine division.
- Royalty payments seemed preferable to a large immediate fee for an unproven men's release.

The fragrance's success did not initially anger Céleste.

It delighted her.

C'est La Reine had finally entered the male market.

Alexandra became wealthy, but Céleste assumed that wealth would bind her more closely to the house which had made it possible.

---

## The Future Céleste Expected

Céleste imagined Alexandra becoming:

- Director of masculine perfumery.
- The creative authority behind an expanding men's catalogue.
- A prestigious younger figure operating beneath the C'est La Reine crown.
- A public symbol of the house's capacity to identify and cultivate genius.
- A successor in creative matters without becoming an independent rival.

Céleste believed she had offered Alexandra the correct future.

She never considered that Alexandra might regard the collaboration as the foundation of her escape.

When Alexandra left and founded Maison Le Veil, Céleste experienced the decision as:

- Ingratitude.
- Disrespect.
- Personal rejection.
- Corporate betrayal.
- The theft of a future Céleste believed she had already designed.
- A public suggestion that C'est La Reine had been useful only as a platform.

The royalty agreement then became intolerable.

Every sale of La Reine de Versailles Pour Homme enriched the founder of a competing house.

---

## The Rivalry

Céleste initially expected Maison Le Veil to fail.

She believed Alec would discover that:

- One successful fragrance did not equal a viable house.
- Retail access depended upon older relationships.
- Investors could be predatory.
- Supply chains rewarded established companies.
- Branding could not substitute for institutional weight.
- Public fascination would eventually move elsewhere.

Céleste hoped failure would bring Alec back.

She imagined offering her a dignified return under stricter terms.

Maison Le Veil succeeded instead.

Alec made several decisions Céleste privately recognised as excellent:

- Rejecting suspicious investors.
- Preserving creative control.
- Building a distinct visual identity.
- Using cylindrical packaging rather than imitating C'est La Reine.
- Rebranding Le Veil rapidly after the phrase "blue rose fragrance" became viral.
- Expanding from La Rose Bleue Voilée into Le Masque and Masquerade.
- Balancing critic-oriented work with commercially accessible releases.
- Retaining the royalty income from La Reine de Versailles Pour Homme while competing directly with its owner.

Each success made reconciliation less likely and humiliation more visible.

---

## Why La Rose Bleue Voilée Hurt Most

Le Masque received greater critical reverence among some fragrance enthusiasts.

Céleste disliked that praise.

La Rose Bleue Voilée frightened her more.

It was:

- More masculine than the other Le Veil Collection fragrances.
- A direct competitor to La Reine de Versailles Pour Homme.
- Broadly wearable.
- Commercially enormous.
- Easy for consumers to understand.
- Built around a viral identity Alec exploited with exceptional speed.
- Supported by the same model who had appeared in C'est La Reine's most discussed masculine campaign.

Rose Blue proved that Alec could reproduce mass-market success outside C'est La Reine.

Céleste could dismiss Le Masque as an enthusiast favourite.

She could not dismiss the sales figures of La Rose Bleue Voilée.

---

## The Model Provocation

The La Rose Bleue Voilée Eau de Toilette launch used the same Black male model featured in:

**Some Crowns Are Earned**

for La Reine de Versailles Pour Homme.

Céleste interpreted the casting as deliberate.

The visual contrast made the insult sharper:

- C'est La Reine had dressed him in black within a dark academic library.
- Maison Le Veil dressed him in white and blue within a bright modern interior.
- He had held the symbolic crown of one fragrance.
- He now held the bottle and mask of its rival.

The public joke was that the model had changed houses.

Céleste saw the campaign as Alec claiming that the masculine identity she created for C'est La Reine belonged more naturally to Maison Le Veil.

The remaining principal models were protected by non-compete clauses.

Maison Le Veil never approached them as far as Céleste knows.

She does not know whether Alec intended a larger sequence or whether the single casting was the complete provocation.

The uncertainty still irritates her.

---

## Escalation One: Watch and Wait

Céleste's earliest response was not illegal.

She monitored:

- Maison Le Veil's investors.
- Retail negotiations.
- Manufacturing partners.
- Press coverage.
- Supplier relationships.
- Alec's public appearances.
- The continuing performance of La Reine de Versailles Pour Homme.
- The launch trajectory of Le Veil.

She expected structural weaknesses to emerge.

They did not.

---

## Escalation Two: False Opportunity

Céleste arranged for apparently independent investors to approach Maison Le Veil.

The offers were designed to create:

- Loss of creative control.
- Unfavourable dilution.
- Dependence upon hostile capital.
- Contractual access to formulas and trademarks.
- Pressure for rapid expansion.
- A later path toward acquisition.

Alec rejected the most dangerous proposals.

Céleste interpreted this as evidence that Alec was either more cautious than expected or receiving advice from someone unusually competent.

That adviser was Vivienne Rousseau.

---

## Escalation Three: Reputation Warfare

Céleste authorised planted tabloid stories concerning Alec's private life.

The stories portrayed Alec as:

- Promiscuous.
- Unstable.
- Difficult to work with.
- Seducing protégées and business contacts.
- Using intimacy to gain access.
- More interested in celebrity than perfumery.
- Careless with professional boundaries.

Some allegations were distorted versions of truth.

Alec had cheated on Margot with Ayo.

She was openly sexual and attracted attention.

The factual fragments made the stories harder to dismiss entirely.

Céleste did not require the public to believe every article.

She wanted investors, retailers, and partners to wonder whether Alec represented unnecessary risk.

---

## Escalation Four: Retail Pressure

Céleste used longstanding relationships to limit Maison Le Veil's access to prestigious counters and department stores.

Methods included:

- Threatening to reduce C'est La Reine allocations.
- Offering exclusivity incentives.
- Pressuring distributors.
- Scheduling conflicting launches.
- Encouraging buyers to treat Maison Le Veil as a temporary celebrity house.
- Suggesting that Alec's royalty income made her financially unreliable as an independent operator.

The pressure slowed some negotiations.

It did not stop Maison Le Veil.

Alec compensated through selective partnerships, direct prestige retail, publicity, and the growing demand for Rose Blue.

---

## Escalation Five: The Failed Blue Response

C'est La Reine developed a masculine blue fragrance intended to reclaim commercial territory from La Rose Bleue Voilée.

The release arrived too late.

Its formula was competent but derivative, and reviewers compared it relentlessly with Rose Blue.

The project failed to produce the desired market correction.

Internally, Céleste blamed:

- Conservative perfumers.
- Slow committees.
- Retail hesitation.
- Maison Le Veil's head start.
- The impossibility of imitating Alec without making the imitation obvious.

Publicly, C'est La Reine treated the release as an independent addition to its masculine catalogue.

---

## Escalation Six: Supply-Chain Collusion

During the development of Masquerade, Céleste escalated from aggressive competition into coordinated corporate sabotage.

She used decades of influence over:

- Major fragrance houses.
- Compounders.
- Raw-material suppliers.
- Bottle manufacturers.
- Contract laboratories.
- Distributors.
- Retail chains.

The plan was to create shortages, sudden compliance concerns, withdrawn agreements, and impossible price changes.

Its objectives were:

1. Delay Masquerade.
2. Force reformulation of the in-development fragrance.
3. Create ingredient instability affecting Le Masque.
4. Create ingredient instability affecting La Rose Bleue Voilée.
5. Damage performance consistency.
6. Produce consumer complaints.
7. Make Maison Le Veil appear too fragile to survive independently.

Céleste did not need every participant to understand the complete scheme.

Most believed they were protecting an older commercial relationship or responding to ordinary corporate pressure.

---

## Vivienne's Invisible Counterattack

The sabotage failed because Vivienne Rousseau removed the roadblocks.

Céleste observed that:

- Suppliers abruptly reversed decisions.
- Financing appeared where it should not have existed.
- Retailers abandoned coordinated pressure.
- Contract manufacturers became unexpectedly cooperative.
- Individuals who had agreed to delay Maison Le Veil changed position without explanation.
- New corporate structures appeared faster than normal due diligence should have permitted.

Céleste concluded that Vivienne possessed a private network stronger than expected.

She did not suspect Dominate, Ventrue influence, or the Camarilla.

The experience frightened her because ordinary corporate logic no longer predicted outcomes.

For the first time, Céleste felt that Alec had acquired a protector whom she could not outmanoeuvre through business alone.

---

## The Decision to Kill Alec

The assassination order was the culmination of frustration, fear, and wounded entitlement.

Several pressures converged:

- Maison Le Veil continued growing.
- Rose Blue remained commercially dominant.
- Masquerade was moving toward launch despite sabotage.
- C'est La Reine continued paying royalties to its competitor.
- Vivienne's intervention made ordinary pressure ineffective.
- Alec showed no intention of returning.
- Céleste feared that Maison Le Veil might eventually eclipse C'est La Reine's masculine identity entirely.
- Every successful sale reminded Céleste that she had financed Alec's independence.

Céleste convinced herself that Alec had become an existential threat to the company.

The language of corporate survival allowed her to cross into murder without naming the decision honestly.

---

## The Conspiracy

Céleste did not personally contact the assassin.

She used two layers of insulation:

1. A trusted corporate fixer who had previously handled private investigations, labour intimidation, and reputational problems.
2. A security intermediary with access to deniable violent contractors.

Only the fixer knew that Céleste had personally authorised the operation.

The security intermediary knew the target and payment structure but not the complete corporate motive.

The assassin knew only that Alec was to be killed quietly at night in an isolated location.

The method was intended to resemble robbery, personal retaliation, or an unidentified private dispute rather than corporate assassination.

The names of the fixer, intermediary, and assassin remain deliberately undefined until they become relevant in play.

---

## The Parking-Area Attack

Alec was ambushed in an isolated parking area at night.

The assassin succeeded in shooting her.

Vivienne intervened before the attack could be completed with certainty.

The assassin escaped the immediate confrontation and reported:

- Alec had been hit.
- Blood loss appeared severe.
- An unidentified woman intervened with extraordinary speed and violence.
- The target was removed before death could be personally confirmed.

Céleste did not receive a body.

She received probability.

She authorised final payment because the available evidence suggested Alec could not survive without immediate extraordinary intervention.

Vivienne provided exactly that intervention.

---

## What Céleste Believes Happened

Céleste does not know Alec became Kindred.

She knows:

- Alec was shot.
- Vivienne intervened.
- Alec disappeared from ordinary public life.
- Maison Le Veil issued controlled signs of life.
- Alec became reclusive and later supposedly left Paris.
- No body, hospital record, or credible public death report appeared.
- Vivienne remained capable of managing the company's continuity.

Céleste's present belief shifts between three possibilities:

1. Alec survived and was permanently injured.
2. Alec died, while Vivienne constructed the appearance of survival to protect Maison Le Veil.
3. Alec survived and deliberately disappeared because the attack frightened her into retreat.

Céleste cannot determine which is true.

This uncertainty is one of her greatest hidden vulnerabilities.

---

## Regret

Céleste regrets losing control of the situation.

She regrets creating evidence.

She regrets relying upon people who can threaten her.

She regrets not understanding Vivienne before escalating.

She does not consistently regret wanting Alec removed.

Her remorse is selective and unstable.

On some nights, she recognises that she attempted to murder a woman she once liked because that woman refused the future Céleste chose for her.

On others, she insists that Alec forced the conflict by converting C'est La Reine's greatest masculine triumph into a weapon against the house.

Céleste's capacity for guilt should remain real.

It should not become immediate redemption.

---

## Surviving Evidence

Céleste destroyed or compartmentalised most direct evidence.

Several dangerous fragments remain:

- A private ledger recording payments to the corporate fixer under false consulting categories.
- Archived messages concerning the timing of Alec's nocturnal movements.
- Supply-chain correspondence revealing coordinated pressure against Maison Le Veil.
- Records connecting planted tabloid stories to a public-relations intermediary.
- A security report describing Vivienne's intervention.
- A final payment authorisation issued after the attack.
- Notes concerning Alec's royalty income and the financial effect of Maison Le Veil's growth.
- Non-compete contract revisions made after the model controversy.

No single fragment proves the complete conspiracy.

Together, they form a path.

---

## The Fixer's Leverage

The unnamed corporate fixer preserved enough documentation to protect themselves.

Céleste knows this.

The fixer has not openly blackmailed her because:

- Céleste continues paying for silence.
- Exposure would implicate the fixer.
- C'est La Reine remains useful.
- The uncertainty surrounding Alec's survival makes the value of the evidence unstable.

If Céleste loses control of the fixer, the conspiracy may surface through mortal law, Kindred investigation, corporate espionage, or extortion.

---

## Relationship to Alec

Céleste's feelings toward Alec remain contradictory.

She feels:

- Pride in having recognised her.
- Resentment that Alec escaped.
- Admiration for Alec's decisions.
- Humiliation at being outmanoeuvred.
- Anger over the royalties.
- Grief for the future she imagined.
- Fear that Alec survived.
- Fear that Alec died.
- A buried desire to justify herself directly to Alec.

Céleste may still think of Alexandra as someone she could have loved professionally, almost maternally, had Alexandra remained within the house.

This makes the betrayal feel more intimate.

It does not make Céleste less responsible.

---

## Relationship to Vivienne

Céleste knows Vivienne as the investor who made Maison Le Veil unnaturally difficult to damage.

She fears Vivienne without understanding her.

Vivienne represents:

- Wealth Céleste could not trace completely.
- Influence without visible institutional origin.
- The collapse of carefully built corporate pressure.
- The woman who appeared at the assassination scene.
- The possibility that Céleste targeted someone protected by forces outside ordinary business.

Céleste has not moved directly against Vivienne since the attack.

That caution is one reason the cover story has remained intact.

---

## Hidden Vulnerability

Céleste's deepest vulnerability is not merely exposure.

It is the possibility that Alec survived and knows.

If Alec returns publicly or confronts her privately, Céleste must decide whether to:

- Deny everything.
- Attempt another removal.
- Bargain.
- Confess selectively.
- Offer evidence against the intermediaries.
- Claim the fixer exceeded instructions.
- Invoke their former relationship.
- Frame the attack as a moment of corporate desperation.
- Seek protection from powers she does not understand.

Céleste is most dangerous when she believes only one route remains.

---

## Long-Term Desire

Céleste wants:

- C'est La Reine to outlive her.
- The royalty burden to end.
- Maison Le Veil contained or absorbed.
- The assassination conspiracy buried.
- Vivienne's influence understood.
- Alec's status confirmed.
- Her public legacy preserved.
- To believe that her worst decision was an aberration rather than the logical result of her need for control.

She may pursue reconciliation only if it also preserves the house.

She is not ready to sacrifice C'est La Reine for moral clarity.

---

## Storyteller Notes

Céleste should not be portrayed as a supernatural mastermind.

She is mortal.

Her danger comes from:

- Corporate legitimacy.
- Money.
- Long relationships.
- Institutional pressure.
- The ability to make harmful actions look like ordinary business.
- A willingness to escalate when control fails.

She is not a cartoonishly jealous perfumer.

She is an executive who converted emotional entitlement into corporate warfare and eventually murder.

Her original affection for Alec was genuine.

Her belief that Alec belonged beneath C'est La Reine was also genuine.

The tragedy is that Céleste could recognise Alec's brilliance without recognising Alec's autonomy.
